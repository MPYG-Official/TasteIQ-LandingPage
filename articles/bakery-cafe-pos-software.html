Bakery & café POS software guide

Updated July 2026 · 14 min read · Bakery & pastry cafés

How bakery cafés should choose POS software when pastry cases, coffee bars, and wholesale trays share one business—and one till.

Bakery & café POS software has to serve two personalities at once. One side is a specialty café: espresso modifiers, short queues, and high ticket velocity. The other is a production bakery: daypart assortments, weight or piece sales, wholesale trays, and waste if afternoon trays do not sell. A generic restaurant POS that ignores that dual reality creates blind spots in inventory and pricing.

What makes bakery café operations distinctive

Morning peaks often combine coffee rush with high pastry volume. Midday may emphasise sandwiches and salads. Evenings skew to leftover discounts or wholesale pickup. Menus change with bake schedules, not with a static quarterly refresh. If updating the POS takes an IT ticket, bakers quietly stop recording what is actually on the case—and reports become fiction.

Food cost visibility also differs. Flour, butter, and chocolate losses show up across many SKUs. Selling by piece from a display case requires fast lookup, clear categories (viennoiserie, cakes, savoury, packaged goods), and staff who can ring a dozen unique items without scrolling forever.

Staff roles diverge too. A barista and a case attendant may share one terminal at peak. The interface must be readable for both without forcing either into the other’s mental model of the menu.

Features bakery cafés should prioritise

Daypart and case-friendly menus

Operators need to enable and disable items as trays empty—without breaking tax or pricing histories. Quick edits for “sold out” and temporary specials keep guest expectations honest. AI-assisted menu setup in TasteIQ helps when photographing trays and drinks to bootstrap a new store or seasonal board.

Coffee modifiers plus pastry simplicity

Drink tickets need milk alternatives, sizes, and add-ons. Pastry tickets often need only quantity and heating notes. Routing should send hot drinks one way and heat-to-order savoury items another when a small kitchen is involved. Confusing KOT design is how croissants get forgotten behind drink queues.

Inventory that respects perishability

Low-stock alerts on milk and packaging matter as much as flour. Tracking wholesale versus retail cans of sales helps owners decide tomorrow’s bake list. Multi-outlet bakery brands need shared recipes with location-level performance—see multi-outlet restaurant management.

Tax-ready bakery invoices

Packaged sweets, dine-in service, and takeaway coffee may require careful category mapping under GST or VAT. Configure tax with professional guidance and enforce it in software. Turning bake lists into POS truth

The best bakery cafés connect production planning to sales data. Yesterday’s mid-morning sell-through should inform today’s tray counts. Your POS will not replace a baker’s judgement, but it should answer three questions quickly:

  1. Which pastry SKUs sold out early, and at what time?
  2. Which trays were discounted or wasted near close?
  3. How did coffee attach rates change when pastry variety changed?

If those answers require exporting three spreadsheets, operators will keep guessing—and food cost will stay fuzzy. Demand reports that separate beverage, fresh pastry, packaged retail, and wholesale without creative filtering.

Comparing bakery POS platforms

Time the transaction and inspect the kitchen/prep output. Then push a mid-afternoon sold-out update and confirm reporting still separates coffee versus pastry sales cleanly.

Wholesale, catering, and prepaid trays

Many bakery cafés supply nearby offices, hotels, or community events. Those orders often need scheduled fulfilment, bulk quantities, and invoices that finance can recognise. Your bakery POS software should capture those sales without forcing them into awkward dine-in tickets that distort average check and food-cost ratios.

Preorders for celebration cakes introduce another wrinkle: deposits, flavour notes, and pickup windows. Even a lightweight workflow—tagged preorder tickets and clear balance due—prevents reverse-queue chaos on Saturdays when walk-in coffee guests and cake pickups collide.

Tag wholesale and catering clearly in reporting. Otherwise owners will believe the café “average ticket” collapsed on a day that was actually dominated by prepaid bulk trays—and make the wrong staffing or pricing decisions.

Seasonality, pop-ups, and second counters

Holiday seasons explode SKU count. Festival specials, limited-run cookies, and gift boxes appear and vanish in weeks. The POS must absorb that volatility without forcing a rebuild of core categories. Pop-up counters at markets or partner hotels need the same menus with different stock levels and tax already correct.

A cloud bakery café POS that runs on tablets lets you open a temporary station without buying proprietary hardware for a three-week window—and still keep sales in the same reporting universe as the mothership café.

Bakery café POS checklist

  • Mixed coffee + pastry tickets complete quickly under rush
  • Sold-out and daypart changes take minutes, not days
  • Reports separate beverage, pastry, and packaged retail
  • Inventory signals prevent empty cases and wasted trays
  • Tax mapping is clear for dine-in, takeaway, and packaged goods
  • Second location can reuse menus without rebuilding from scratch
  • Wholesale or catering tickets do not distort café reporting
  • Preorder or cake pickup notes survive a busy Saturday queue
