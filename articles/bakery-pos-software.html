How bakery POS and order management should handle case sell-downs, custom cakes, wholesale trays, and café add-ons without losing production notes.

Bakery POS software manages more than counter sales. Bakeries sell from the display case, take custom cake preorders, fulfill wholesale trays, and often run a small café window on the side. Order management, production notes, pickup timing, and tax-ready billing have to stay in one workflow—or staff invent parallel notebooks that become the real system of record.

Why bakeries need POS beyond a simple till

A loaf sold at 8 a.m. and a three-tier wedding cake picked up Saturday are both sales—but they need different notes, deposits, and production calendars. Generic restaurant POS often lacks space for pickup dates, icing preferences, and photo references without dumping everything into an unstructured comment field that never reaches the baking team.

Case-driven retail also differs from table service. Guests browse, ask weights or flavours, and decide quickly. Staff must mark sold-out items, move day-old discounts, and keep GST or VAT correct for packaged goods versus café service where applicable. Bakery POS features that matter

Case categories and rapid sell-downs

Morning boards change as trays empty. Bakery POS software should make 86’ing items and marking discounts easy so cashiers do not sell products that no longer exist on the shelf.

Custom cake and preorder management

Capture flavour, size, message, deposit, balance due, and pickup window. Tickets should surface to bakers with enough lead time—not disappear into a chat group. When cafés attach, see also bakery & café POS software.

Wholesale and catering tickets

Tray orders for corporates or hotels should not distort retail café reports. Separate customer types or order tags keep margin analysis honest.

Production-aware inventory

Flour, butter, eggs, and packaging behave differently from plated restaurant ingredients. Pair POS depletion with practical bakery inventory habits—detail in bakery inventory management.

Order management workflows to script before buying

  1. Walk-in case sale with mixed SKUs and a quick discount.
  2. Custom cake deposit today, balance at pickup, with frosting note.
  3. Wholesale tray order for next-day delivery with packing list.
  4. Café add-on drink and pastry on the same ticket if you run a dual model.
  5. Partial refund when a preorder is cancelled with deposit rules.

If a vendor cannot stage those five scenarios on your SKUs, the “bakery POS” label is marketing veneer.

Seasonal calendars and bakery demand spikes

Diwali trays, Christmas yule logs, Eid cookies, Valentine cakes, and wedding season create order volume that casual café POS tools rarely survive. Bakery POS software should support deposit schedules, pickup calendars that bakers can print or filter, and capacity warnings when a finishing bench is already overloaded. Without capacity awareness, yes-saying becomes brand damage when cakes arrive late.

Capture allergen and message fields carefully. A misspelled name on a celebration cake is not a cute story—it is a refund and a trust scar. Structured fields plus photo attachment options beat free-text scrolling on a phone at midnight. Training bakery cashiers for case psychology

Case selling is consultative for thirty seconds and transactional for the next ten. Staff need quick weight or piece pricing, combo prompts that raise attach rate, and clear sold-out flags. If the interface hides bestsellers behind deep folders, guests leave with less than they intended and Instagramming competitors win the next impulse buy.

Include refund and remake drills in training. Damaged pastries and incorrect custom cakes happen. Managers need consistent policies encoded in POS—partial refunds, remake without double revenue, and waste coding that feeds inventory wastage reviews described in the inventory guide.

Second outlets fail when menus diverge silently. Central catalogues with outlet-level availability (some shops lack ovens for certain SKUs) keep brand price coherence while respecting kitchen reality.
