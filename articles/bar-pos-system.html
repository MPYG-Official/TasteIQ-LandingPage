Bar POS

Bar POS system for billing, KOT, and inventory

Updated July 2026 · 12 min read · Bars & nightlife

What bar POS software must deliver for cocktail speed, station KOT, open tabs, and inventory that survives a Saturday night rush.

A bar POS system handles billing, KOT or bar tickets, and inventory in an environment where tickets move fast, modifiers pile up, and stock leakage can erase an entire night’s margin. Pubs, cocktail bars, and hotel bars need speed without sacrificing pour control, tax correctness, and clear station routing between bar and kitchen.

This guide is for bar owners and F&B managers evaluating bar POS software in 2026. It covers billing, KOT, and inventory together—because treating them as three vendor silos is how midnight reconciliations fail. Explore TasteIQ and pricing as you compare options.

What makes bar POS different from café or QSR tills

Bars mix open tabs, sequential pours, kitchen food, happy-hour discounts, and sometimes bottle service. Staff rotate quickly on weekend nights. If ringing a classic cocktail with a spirit swap takes more than a few taps, guests wait and bartenders invent paper side systems.

Inventory is another differentiator. Free-pour culture without measured recipes makes theoretical stock fantasy. Even measured bars lose margin when transfers between cellar and speed rail are undocumented. POS must support recipe-level depletion where you are ready for it—and at least bottle-level receiving and wastage codes when you are not.

Billing capabilities bars should demand

Fast cocktails and modifiers

Base spirit swaps, garnish notes, spicy or smoky variations, and shot add-ons need structured modifiers. Happy hour pricing must flip and reverse cleanly with an audit trail.

Tabs, splits, and settlements

Open tabs with room for sequential adds, split-by-item or split-even, and clear settlement modes (card, UPI, cash, room charge in hotel venues) prevent end-of-night arguments.

Tax and service charge clarity

Indian bars often combine GST treatments for food and alcohol categories with service charge. Configure categories carefully; treat this page as operational guidance, not legal advice. Compliance setup for food businesses starts with journeys like FSSAI licensing; liquor permissions are jurisdiction-specific and typically need specialised counsel beyond software.

KOT and station routing for bar + kitchen

Many bars sell small plates alongside drinks. Bar tickets should reach the cocktail station while food KOT reaches the kitchen without bartenders walking tickets. Wrong routing creates cold fries and warm cocktails for the wrong guests. TasteIQ-style kitchen tickets keep stations aligned when menus span both.

Reprint and void behaviour matters after midnight. Managers need locked reasons and visible trails so comps do not become an informal tip system for friends.

Bar inventory and wastage control essentials

  • Bottle receiving against invoices with brand and size accuracy
  • Transfers from cellar to bar documented
  • Recipe depletion for high-volume cocktails where feasible
  • Spill, breakage, tasting, and complimentary reason codes
  • Variance reviews after peak nights—not monthly surprises

Inventory without billing context is incomplete; billing without inventory context invites silent leaks. Keep both in one operational stack.

Demo checklist for bar POS software

  1. Ring three complex cocktails with spirit swaps under thirty seconds each.
  2. Open a tab, add food and drinks, split the bill two ways, settle with two tenders.
  3. Route bar versus kitchen tickets and reprint one after a remake.
  4. Apply happy-hour, then reverse it, and inspect void/comp logs.
  5. Receive two bottles, transfer one, and review variance after simulated pours.

Happy hour, comps, and midnight controls

Promotion windows are where bar POS integrity is tested. Automatic happy-hour price flips must reverse on schedule. Manager comps need PIN or role locks with reason codes. Without those controls, “friends and family” becomes an unstructured COGS event every Friday. Review void and comp reports the morning after peak nights while memories are fresh.

Staff meal and tasting policies should be written and rung, not whispered. Unrecorded tastings look identical to leakage in variance reports and poison culture. TasteIQ soft pitch: clean trails turn difficult conversations into coaching instead of accusation.

Security, theft patterns, and camera-adjacent ops

Bar inventory is high-value and portable. Combine POS variance with basic physical controls: sealed spirit cabinets, limited cellar keys, and spot checks against camera timelines when discrepancies spike. Software alone does not replace management presence, but without POS data you are guessing in the dark.

For hotel bars, room-charge postings and folio clarity matter as much as cellar locks. Confirm with vendors how F&B charges settle when guests are in-house. Property context lives on the TasteIQ hotels pages linked earlier.

Building a cocktail programme the POS can support

Menu engineering collapses when the till cannot distinguish high-margin signatures from loss-leader shots. Tag cocktails by category, track modifiers that change cost (top-shelf swaps), and retire clutter SKUs that slow training. A tight menu with perfect tickets beats a sprawling list staff fear to ring.
