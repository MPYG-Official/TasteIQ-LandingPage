1. Clarify the café concept before you sign a lease

Decide what kind of café you are actually opening: specialty coffee bar, bakery-café hybrid, coworking-friendly lounge, or quick-service takeaway. Concept drives rent budget, kitchen build-out, seating mix, and ticket size. A 12-seat espresso bar with strong takeaway can outperform a large, underfilled lounge if location and labour are misaligned.

Write a one-page concept brief: dayparts (morning rush vs evening), average ticket target, top twenty SKUs, and whether you will do delivery from day one. Investors, landlords, and future managers will ask for this clarity; building it early also simplifies menu engineering and POS category design later.

2. Location, lease, and fit-out realities

Walk the street at peak times. Count footfall, note competition, and check power, water, waste disposal, and grease-trap expectations with the landlord. Negotiate fit-out timelines and rent-free periods so construction delays do not burn cash before the first sale.

Confirm zoning and premises use for foodservice. Many café projects stall when the owner assumes a retail or office lease automatically allows a commercial kitchen. Budget furniture, counters, espresso machine, grinders, refrigeration, and ventilation as a connected project—not isolated shopping trips.

Rent vs ticket size

High-street rent needs high conversion or takeaway volume. Model breaks-even before aesthetic upgrades.

Fit-out overruns

Plumbing, exhaust, and electrical rework are the usual budget killers. Keep a contingency line.

Licence sequencing

Some approvals need premises photos or NOCs. Start paperwork as soon as possession is firm.

Opening without POS

Notebooks and spreadsheets fail on day three. Choose billing before soft launch.

3. Compliance stack: FSSAI, GST, and local licenses

In India, most cafés need food business registration or licence under FSSAI, GST registration (when thresholds or interstate/supplier needs apply), Shop & Establishment registration, and often local health or trade licences. Fire NOC requirements depend on size and seating. Exact documents and timelines differ by state and municipal body.

Deep-dive checklists:

  • FSSAI license for restaurants & cafés
  • GST registration for F&B businesses
  • Shop & Establishment / trade license
  • Fire NOC & health/trade license checklist

Common mistakes: applying under the wrong FSSAI category, mismatched address documents, delaying GST until supplier invoices pile up, and opening soft-launch weeks without invoice numbering discipline. Build a shared folder for PAN, Aadhaar/director KYC, lease deed or rent agreement, photos, and layout sketches.

5. Menu design that cashiers can actually ring

Start with a focused menu: eight to twelve signature drinks, a short food or pastry list, and one or two seasonal specials. Excess SKUs create training pain, waste, and inconsistent tickets. Price for food cost, labour, rent, and payment fees—not only competitor screenshots.

Define modifier rules for milk alternatives, sweetness, and add-ons. Every modifier should map to a POS button and a recipe cost. Photograph dishes and drinks for ordering screens and for AI-assisted menu setup if you use TasteIQ’s onboarding tools.

6. Staffing, suppliers, and soft launch

Hire for the bar first: at least one person who can pull consistent shots and one who can own hospitality at the till. Write standard recipes, opening/closing checklists, and cash reconciliation steps. Set up milk, bean, and pastry suppliers with backup options; single-source shortages kill cafés in the first month.

Run a friends-and-family soft launch for three to five days. Measure ticket times, remakes, inventory variances, and guest confusion. Adjust before announcing a grand opening. Keep a weekly owner dashboard: sales by daypart, waste, labour hours, and top movers.

Café opening checklist (printable summary)

  1. Concept brief, unit economics, and cash buffer for three months of fixed costs.
  2. Location shortlist, lease negotiation, fit-out scope with contingency.
  3. FSSAI application path started; GST decision documented; local trade/shop licences tracked.
  4. Equipment list, supplier accounts, and insurance conversations underway.
  5. Menu locked for soft launch; TasteIQ (or equivalent POS) configured and trained.
  6. Staff scheduled with shadow shifts; soft launch completed; marketing live.

Outside India

Café journeys abroad often replace FSSAI/GST with local food permits, VAT/GST systems, and liquor rules if you serve alcohol. The operating sequence still holds: concept → premises → compliance → POS → menu → staff → soft launch.
